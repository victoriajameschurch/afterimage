Memory, perception, consciousness, and the strange ways the brain turns experience into something we carry.

BEFORE WE BEGIN

You probably don't remember most of yesterday.

You remember pieces.

Maybe the smell of someone's perfume. A sentence someone said. The exact color of the sky. Sitting somewhere in the backseat. The feeling of being eight years old and realizing something for the first time.

The strange thing is that your brain didn't choose these moments at random.

Memory is selective. It is reconstructive. It is influenced by emotion, attention, expectation, context, and everything that happens after the original event.

01 / MEMORY IS RECONSTRUCTION

Remembering isn't replaying. It's rebuilding.

When you remember an event, your brain doesn't simply retrieve an untouched recording. Different pieces of information are brought back together.

This helps explain why memories can change over time, and why two people can experience the same event but later remember it differently.

02 / EMOTION

Why do some moments refuse to disappear?

Emotion can influence what gets remembered and how strongly it is remembered.

Brain systems involving the amygdala, hippocampus, and prefrontal cortex play important roles in emotional memory.

Sometimes the emotional part of an event survives even when the details become blurry.

03 / THE SMELL OF THE PAST

Why can a smell suddenly take you somewhere you haven't been in years?

Smell has an unusual relationship with autobiographical memory.

A particular scent can become associated with a person, place, or period of life. Encountering that scent years later can bring the associated memory back with surprising vividness.

04 / FALSE MEMORIES

A memory can feel completely real and still be wrong.

Human memory is not perfectly accurate. Research has shown that people can sometimes remember details that did not occur, or become confident in memories that have been altered.

Feeling certain about something is not always the same as being correct about it.

05 / CHILDHOOD

Why can't we remember being two?

Most adults have very few autobiographical memories from their earliest years. This is often called childhood or infantile amnesia.

That doesn't mean nothing was happening. Young children are learning language, building a sense of self, and developing systems that support autobiographical memory.

Your earliest years happened. You just may not have a story you can consciously tell yourself about them.

06 / TIME

Why does childhood feel so long?

Time itself doesn't necessarily speed up as we grow older. Our experience of time is influenced by attention, emotion, novelty, and memory.

A period filled with new experiences can create many distinct memories. Looking backward, that period may therefore seem larger or more substantial than a familiar stretch of time filled with routine.

Maybe that's part of why childhood can feel enormous when you're inside it and strangely small when you look back.

07 / CONSCIOUSNESS

But who is doing the remembering?

Memory raises a much stranger question: what exactly is the self that experiences these memories?

Neuroscience can study brain activity, attention, perception, memory, and consciousness. But there are still fundamental questions about subjective experience that science has not completely answered.

We can describe what happens in the brain when you see a color. We can study what happens when you remember a person. But explaining why any of it feels like something from the inside is a much harder problem.

08 / WHY AFTERIMAGE ASKS QUESTIONS

The questions aren't supposed to have answers.

AFTERIMAGE asks about memory, consciousness, perception, time, space, and existence because these things sit at the edge of what we understand.

Some questions have scientific explanations. Some have competing theories. Some are philosophical. And some may not have an answer at all.

That's the point.

The archive isn't trying to prove what people remember. It's interested in what people choose to remember, what they wonder about, and what their answers reveal about the way human beings experience being alive.
